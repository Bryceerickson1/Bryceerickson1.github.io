Class project · Future of Work

Auditing Performance Review Language with a Four-Agent AI Workflow

I built a four-step AI workflow that turns a manager’s informal notes into a formal appraisal, audits its language, and prepares the manager for the review conversation. A planned Python scanner would add a rule-based second check.

Highlights

  • Tested whether a job title alone changes how AI judges an employee.
  • Found AI most useful questioning a review, not writing it.
  • Caught AI inventing convincing facts, a serious HR risk.

My contribution

I adapted class-supplied prompts by adding two rules to all four agents: weigh the job title, and analyze every input fresh. I ran the notes as Janitor, Marketing Coordinator, and Artist Entrepreneur, and set up the scanner’s tools.

How it works

  1. Agent 1 sorts notes into four categories.
  2. Agent 2 writes the appraisal.
  3. Agent 3 audits its language.
  4. Agent 4 prepares a meeting guide.

I carry each output forward by hand. The scanner would check whether praise is as specific as criticism.

Finding

The manager wrote that the employee “needs to work on being more of a team player.” The appraisal turned this into a formal criticism behind a middle rating, though the notes never described a moment of poor teamwork. Both audits caught the gap.

The concern may be real, but the record cannot show it, and an employee cannot answer or improve on a criticism without an example.

Managers should document a real incident or limit the review to what is provable: late updates and two missed meetings.

Limitations

Each title ran once, and my personal Claude settings shaped some outputs, so title differences are a lead for testing, not a conclusion.

What I learned about AI workflows

Narrow roles worked: later agents caught earlier agents’ errors, from vague claims to leftover chat text. The workflow’s real value was interrogation, showing a manager what to verify before a hard conversation. That strength carries a risk: one audit invented sample figures convincing enough to enter a real record, so every fact needs a human check.
